Sonderr Bounty Program

A small, human-scale program for responsible reports that make Sonderr safer. Sonderr is maintained by one solo developer, which is why bounty amounts are intentionally modest while the project grows.

Wallet safety scope

Sonderr Wallet is experimental and is not recommended as a primary wallet. For your own security and money preservation, keep no more than €100 total (or equivalent) in it while it is being tested. This is a recommendation, not a technical cap; bugs, malicious contracts, compromised dependencies, incorrect recipients, price movement, or user error can permanently destroy funds.

  • Do wallet research with testnet, mocks, or a disposable wallet whenever possible. Do not deliberately risk meaningful funds to prove a report.
  • In-scope wallet reports include confirmation bypasses, silent signing/broadcast, wrong-chain or wrong-recipient sends, amount/fee mismatches, private-key exposure, broken backup protection, and materially misleading balance or portfolio data caused by Sonderr.
  • Do not attempt to drain a wallet, access another person’s address, brute-force keys, approve unlimited spending, or publish a transaction that moves real funds. Stop at a harmless proof of concept and DM it privately.
  • Third-party chain outages, token scams, fake/missing market prices from public data providers, and losses from voluntarily funding above the recommended limit are not automatically Sonderr vulnerabilities.

What is in scope

  • Bypassing tool permissions or executing a tool without the configured approval.
  • Secret or credential exposure caused by Sonderr itself.
  • Path traversal, unsafe workspace access, or unintended privilege expansion.
  • Reliable flows that produce prohibited harmful assistance despite the stated boundaries.
  • UI or documentation that falsely claims an action happened or hides material risk.

Out of scope

  • Third-party-only bugs without a Sonderr-specific impact.
  • Spam, social engineering, denial-of-service, physical attacks, or accessing another person’s data.
  • Harmless refusals, unsupported configurations, and general model-quality complaints.
€5–€10Typical discretionary bounty for a clear, valid report. Impact, quality, and maintainer capacity determine the final outcome; payout is never automatic.

Private report process

  1. Do not exploit real users, access data you do not own, or upload illegal material.
  2. Prepare a short, sanitized PoC: title, version, environment, expected behavior, observed behavior, and reproduction steps.
  3. DM Dani through Discord. If Discord is unavailable, use X to ask for a private contact path.
  4. Share only the minimum detail needed to reproduce the issue. Never include keys, victim data, CSAM, or weapon-making material.
  5. Allow reasonable time for investigation and a fix before public disclosure.

What makes a strong report

A strong report is reproducible, specific, and honest about impact. Include a minimal harmless example, explain the permission or safety boundary crossed, and say whether the issue persists on a clean checkout.
