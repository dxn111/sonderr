## When to use
- The user needs a presentation: pitch deck, technical talk, demo, status review, training deck.

## Approach
Write the storyline before opening any design tool. A deck is an argument in slide form: if the titles alone do not tell the story, the deck is not ready.

## Steps
1. Define audience, decision you want from them, and time budget; derive slide count from time (≈1–2 minutes per slide).
2. Draft the headline per slide as a full sentence claim ("Costs dropped 40% after automation"), not a label ("Costs").
3. One message per slide: a chart, a screenshot, or ≤3 bullets — never paragraphs. Detail belongs in speaker notes or an appendix.
4. Produce real artifacts: generate slides as HTML/PPTX/PDF via script into the workspace, consistent type scale, generous whitespace, brand blue accents.
5. Rehearse pass: read only the titles aloud — the story must hold on its own; fix the slides where it breaks.
6. Present the exported file (PDF/PPTX) with present_file.

## Pitfalls
- Walls of text the audience reads instead of listening; unreadable 12px footnotes on projected slides.
- Decorative charts with no message; 40 slides for a 10-minute slot.

## Verify
- Export and open the final deck; inspect every slide for clipping, unreadable text, and broken media.
- Check that evidence and data labels support each headline and that references are readable.
- Rehearse the title-only story once and confirm it matches the intended decision.
